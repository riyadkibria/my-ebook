## Centering the Average


The more statistically correct way to plot a moving average is to center it. That means to place it in the middle of the time period it covers. A 10 day average, for example, would be placed five days back. A 20 day average would be plotted 10 days back in time. Centering the average, however, has the major flaw of producing much later trend change signals. Therefore, moving averages are usually placed at the end of the time period covered instead of the middle. The centering technique is used almost exclusively by cyclic analysts to isolate underlying market cycles.
