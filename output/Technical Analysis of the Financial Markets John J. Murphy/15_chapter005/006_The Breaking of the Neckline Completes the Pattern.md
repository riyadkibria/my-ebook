## The Breaking of the Neckline Completes the Pattern


By this time, a flatter trendline can be drawn under the last two reaction lows (points B and D), which is called a neckline (see line 2). This line generally has a slight upward slope at tops (although it’s sometimes horizontal and, less often, tilts downward). The deciding factor in the resolution of the head and shoulders top is a decisive closing violation of that neckline. The market has now violated the trendline along the bottom of points B and D, has broken under support at point D, and has completed the requirement for a new downtrend—descending peaks and troughs. The new downtrend is now identified by the declining highs and lows at points C, D, E, and F. Volume should increase on the breaking of the neckline. A sharp increase in downside volume, however, is not critically important in the initial stages of a market top.
